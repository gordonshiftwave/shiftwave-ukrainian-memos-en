Alona Tolmachova, Director of Correctional Groups, Rivnovaha Psychological Assistance Center, Ukraine. Works with children with autism ages 4.5 to 10.

How her team uses the 10-minute Adapted for Children program before or after lessons, with individual vibration settings and a no-forcing approach.

English transcript: Memo 1, Alona Tolmachova

Good afternoon. I am Alona Tolmachova, director of the correctional groups at the Rivnovaha (Equilibrium) Psychological Assistance Center.

I want to share our team’s experience using Shiftwave in work with children with autism.

Most often we use a 10-minute program in Ukrainian called “Adapted for Children.”

We run the session either before a lesson or after it. Before a lesson, our goal is to help the child engage in interaction, lower anxiety, and better sense their own body; after a lesson, to support recovery after the load.

We adjust vibration intensity individually. In our practice, levels up to 50% were more often used to reduce tension, and above 70% were in some cases used for activation before task performance.

This is a description of our experience, not universal settings for every child. We work with children in correctional groups from 4.5 to 10 years old.

We more often noticed practical benefit in children with tension, motor agitation, or difficulty transitioning into organized activity. For children with tactile hypersensitivity, it is convenient that the chair provides bodily sensations of direct contact with a specialist.

At the same time, tolerance varies. Some children refuse the headphones but respond well to the vibration. So refusing the audio accompaniment does not yet mean the whole procedure does not suit the child. If the vibration itself causes discomfort, that needs to be taken into account. Many children refuse the eye mask. In that case the child may keep their eyes open or close them, whichever is more comfortable.

For us it is essential not to force the child, not to ignore nonverbal signs of discomfort, and not to transfer successful settings from one child to another.

The main takeaway: it is important to consider sensory characteristics, individual reactions, and the child’s state on that particular day. We use Shiftwave as an auxiliary component of comprehensive work.

To help a greater number of children, we proposed developing recommendations on gradual familiarization with the chair, separate adjustment of sound and vibration, and a simple observation form before and after the session.

It is worth systematically recording settings, children’s reactions, and changes after sessions, summarizing them, analyzing data received from specialists, and also separately studying the reactions of children with different sensory profiles.

Hello, my name is Nataliia Yashunina. I am a psychologist, and I work at the Rivnovaha (Equilibrium) Psychological Center in Kyiv.

I have a little experience using the Shiftwave chair in my work, specifically in an integrative way. I do neuropsychological correction with children, I do Floortime, and after the session we go and do a session in the chair.

Which children do I work with? Most often these are children diagnosed with ASD (autism spectrum disorder) or ADHD. To describe their symptoms, most often they have difficulty with self-regulation, frustration, tantrums, and take a long time to calm themselves down.

These difficulties come with, and are combined with, differences in how the nervous system works in their sensory profile. Most often I see hyposensitivity of the proprioceptive system, hyper- or hyposensitivity of the vestibular modality, weak postural control, and a weak sense of interoceptive signals, which affect both attention and the quality of contact.

Some of the children we did Shiftwave with had speech delay. These are children aged four and older.

How do children react to the visit and to the chair therapy? Some sit down easily and relax right away. Some children need more time, even several sessions, from one to three, to sit in the chair longer and tolerate these sensations.

Some may refuse the headphones or the eye mask, possibly because of tactile sensitivity to being touched on the head, or sensitivity to sounds.

What I observe: children may place particular parts of their body right on the little circles that the vibration comes from. Children often lift their legs to stand with their feet on the circles, or press their palms to them, and some even wanted to put their cheek on them, further exploring this movement.

The reactions we see most often: children tune in to the sensations. Some may even sort of go still, quiet down a little. Some react with a smile, start laughing, or start moving more. Some may visibly relax, as if sinking back into the chair.

After the session, some children come out collected, regulated, and calm.

I also want to say that some children need only a specialist with them, while some who are more anxious may need a parent present.

Thank you.

English transcript: Memo 5, Yuliia Markus

Good afternoon, my name is Yuliia Markus, and I am a psychologist at the Rivnovaha (Equilibrium) Psychological Assistance Center in the city of Kyiv, Ukraine.

I want to share our experience using the Shiftwave chair in our work with children and teenagers with autism spectrum disorders.

As for the protocols we use: most often it is the ten-minute program in Ukrainian that is adapted for children.

We use the chair either before the main lesson, to make it easier for the child to get engaged in the work, or after an intense load, for recovery and to reduce tension.

We choose the intensity individually, depending on the child's state and their reaction to the procedure. Lower levels, in particular up to 5 to 10% (unclear in the audio), we use more often specifically to reduce tension, and in some cases an intensity above 70% for activation.

This is not a universal recommendation, but specifically our practical observations.

We work with children of different ages, including preschool children and teenagers. Our experience with teenagers has been especially interesting and useful for us, because it is not always comfortable to offer them movement exercises or other forms of direct bodily stimulation. But this kind of technological equipment is very interesting to them.

Let me give one example. A teenager with autism came to our sessions twice a week for four months, after a school day at a mainstream school. He was already exhausted, and it was hard for him to focus and quickly engage in interaction with the psychologist. After a ten-minute Shiftwave session, we observed reduced motor tension, faster establishment of contact, better uptake of instructions, and longer sustained attention while doing tasks.

At the same time, children's reactions are very individual. Some children accept both the vibration and the audio accompaniment well. Others refuse the headphones, for example, but respond positively to the vibration itself.

In our opinion, the chair can be especially useful for children who seek deep bodily sensations and have difficulty with self-regulation, or who come to sessions after sensory overload. At the same time, for children with high sensitivity to sound or vibration, the procedure may not be suitable, or may require significant adaptation or gradually getting the child used to it.

We can say for certain that you must not force a child to stay in the chair, ignore their nonverbal signals of discomfort, or automatically apply the same settings to everyone. You need to start gradually, watch the reactions, and if needed turn off the audio accompaniment or stop the procedure.

And our main conclusion from using Shiftwave: it is a very powerful auxiliary tool in comprehensive work with children with autism.

To help more children, it is important to study which sensory profile the chair suits best, to systematically record results, and to compare children's reactions under different settings.

And most important: to see first of all the specific child, their specific needs, their comfort, and their readiness to interact.

Thank you, have a good day!

А ось таке технологічне обладнання їм дуже цікаве.

Наведу один приклад. Підліток з аутизмом приходив до нас на заняття два рази на тиждень протягом чотирьох місяців, після навчального дня у загальній школі. І він був уже виснаженим, йому було складно зосередитись та швидко включитися у взаємодію з психологом. Після десятихвилинного сеансу Shiftwave ми спостерігали зменшення рухового напруження, швидше встановлення контакту, краще сприймання інструкцій та довше утримання уваги під час виконання завдань.

Водночас реакція дітей є дуже індивідуальна. Частина дітей добре сприймає і вібрацію, і аудіосупровід. А деякі відмовляються від навушників, наприклад, але позитивно реагують на саму вібрацію.

На нашу думку, крісло може бути особливо корисним дітям, які шукають саме глибокі тілесні відчуття і мають труднощі з саморегуляцією, або приходять на заняття після сенсорного перевантаження. Водночас дітям з високою чутливістю до звуку чи вібрації процедура може не підходити або потребувати суттєвої адаптації чи поступового привчання дітей до такої процедури.

Ми точно можемо сказати, що не можна примушувати дитину залишатися в кріслі, ігнорувати її невербальні сигнали дискомфорту чи автоматично застосовувати однакові параметри до всіх. Потрібно починати поступово, спостерігати за реакціями та за потреби вимикати аудіосупровід або припиняти процедуру.

І наш головний висновок з використання Shiftwave: це дуже потужний допоміжний інструмент у комплексній роботі з дітьми з аутизмом.

Щоб допомогти більшій кількості дітей, важливо досліджувати, якому саме сенсорному профілю крісло підходить найкраще, систематично фіксувати результати та порівнювати реакції дітей за різних параметрів.

І найважливіше: бачити перш за все конкретну дитину, її конкретні потреби, комфорт та готовність до взаємодії.

Practical Experience with the Use of Shiftwave Technology in Working with Children with Autism Spectrum Disorders: Clinical Observations and Possible Neurophysiological Mechanisms

A summary of how the Center's psychologists use Shiftwave before therapy, to help children engage, and after intensive sessions, to help them recover. It covers vibration settings, two case observations, possible mechanisms, and the limits of the observations.

Key points

Shiftwave is used as an add-on to psychotherapy, neuropsychology, sensory integration, play therapy, and bioacoustic work, not as a standalone treatment.

Many children arrive exhausted after school, or after sleepless nights in bomb shelters in Kyiv. A short session beforehand was often followed by less tension, faster engagement, longer attention, and better understanding of instructions.

Intensities up to 50% were more often linked with calming. Over 70% was sometimes used to raise alertness before tasks. These are not universal settings.

Case 1: a teenager with autism who came after school showed less tension, faster contact, and longer attention after a 10-minute session.

Case 2: a four-year-old with serious toileting difficulties almost fully moved to independent toilet use during a combined program that included Shiftwave.

The report is careful to say these are clinical observations, with no control group or standardized measures, and calls for further systematic research.
